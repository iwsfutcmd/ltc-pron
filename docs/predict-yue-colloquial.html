A colloquial layer for predict_yue

Wiktionary predicts one Cantonese reading for each Middle Chinese reading, and that reading is the literary one. This page explains how that prediction is built, describes a new predict_yue_coll that adds the colloquial (白讀) layer, and measures how many more of Wiktionary's attested readings it accounts for.

Readings of 重紐 finals carry a suffix such as -重鈕 (1,982 readings), which infer_categories folds into the division. Entering-tone finals are first renamed to their 入聲 rhyme (庚 → 陌 when the tone is 入).

infer_categories in Module:ltc-pron reduces the string to three integers: one of 38 initials, one of 160 final types (final × division × openness, as far as each final distinguishes them), and a tone from 1 to 4. Both predictors, predict_cmn and predict_yue, take only those three numbers.

How predict_yue builds a syllable

The function works in an internal notation where capital letters mark vowel quality and length, and only spells the result out in Jyutping at the end. It runs five stages, in this order. The colloquial layer hooks into three of them, marked below.

  1. Initial

    initial_outcomes_yue maps the 38 initials to Jyutping onsets. Overrides follow in priority order:

    • Labials become f before the 16 finals that caused 輕唇化 (e.g. 分 fan1).
    • Voiced obstruents in the level tone devoice to aspirates: b→p, d→t, z→c, g→k (平 ping4).
    • 溪 k becomes h before nearly every final (160 of the 161 entries in debuccalisation_inducing_yue), as in 客 hak1 and 開 hoi1. Several initials become j before front glides.
    Colloquial hook 1. Devoicing to an aspirate also applies when a fully voiced initial meets the rising tone (全濁上), giving 重 cung5 instead of zung6.

  2. Rhyme

    final_class_yue folds the 160 final types into 40 rhyme classes. Classes 1–20 have one outcome. Classes 21–34 have three outcomes, chosen by place of articulation (labial, coronal, velar/laryngeal). In 模 that gives 布 bou3, 都 dou1, 古 gu2. Classes 35–40 have eight outcomes, one per initial class. Open 支脂之微 give 比 bei2, 地 dei6, 知 zi1, 子 zi2, 機 gei1, 衣 ji1. Four special cases come first, e.g. 疑 + 模 gives the syllabic 五 ng5.

  3. Onset adjustments from the rhyme

    疑 ng becomes j before high vowels. Before the 60 rounded (合口) finals, h becomes f, a zero or j onset becomes w, and g/k become gw/kw, unless the rhyme vowel is already rounded.

    Colloquial hook 2. After these adjustments, three rhyme replacements: 梗攝 division II aN→AN, open 梗攝 III/IV IN→eN, and 脂 after 心 i→ei. Placing them here keeps 疑 as j before a former high vowel: 迎 jing4 → jeng4.

  4. Tone

    The four Middle Chinese tones split by voicing. A voiceless entering tone splits again by vowel length: long vowels go to tone 3 (中入), short vowels to tone 1. Voiced entering is tone 6. A fully voiced initial in the rising tone moves to tone 6 (濁上變去). Other voiced syllables add 3 (陽平 4, 陽上 5, 陽去 6).

    Colloquial hook 3. 全濁上 keeps 陽上 tone 5. The long-vowel test now also counts e, so colloquial 尺 comes out cek3. No literary rhyme starts with e in the entering tone, so literary output is unchanged.

  5. Spell-out

    om merges into am. Final -m becomes -n after labial onsets (凡 faan4). In the entering tone the nasal codas become stops (-m -n -N → -p -t -k). The internal letters are then spelled in Jyutping:

The colloquial layer

The body of predict_yue is now a local function, yue_reflex(initial, final, tone, colloquial). predict_yue calls it with colloquial = false. The new predict_yue_coll calls it both ways and returns the colloquial form only when it differs, so a display can leave the cell empty where the two layers agree.

-- Colloquial (白讀) layer: 梗攝 rimes keep a low vowel, giving 2nd-division
-- -aang/-aak (生 saang1, 白 baak6) and open 3rd/4th-division -eng/-ek
-- (病 beng6, 石 sek6) beside literary -ang/-ak and -ing/-ik.
local colloquial_geng_2_yue = { [109] = true, [110] = true, [113] = true, [114] = true,
	[117] = true, [118] = true, [119] = true, [120] = true }
local colloquial_geng_34_yue = { [111] = true, [115] = true, [121] = true,
	[123] = true, [125] = true, [127] = true }

local function yue_reflex(initial, final, tone, colloquial)
	local initial_result = initial_outcomes_yue[initial]
	local voiced_rising = colloquial and tone == 2 and fully_voiced_initial[initial]	…
	elseif (tone == 1 or voiced_rising) and devoicing_initial_yue[initial] then	…
	if colloquial then
		if colloquial_geng_2_yue[final] and final_result == "aN" then
			final_result = "AN"
		elseif colloquial_geng_34_yue[final] and final_result == "IN" then
			final_result = "eN"
		-- 脂 after 心 only: 四 sei3, 死 sei2, 肆 sei3.
		elseif final == 15 and initial == 16 and final_result == "i" then
			final_result = "ei"
		end
	end	…
		elseif mw.ustring.find(final_result, "^[AiouOYe]") ~= nil then	…
	elseif tone == 2 and fully_voiced_initial[initial] and not voiced_rising then	…
end

function export.predict_yue(initial, final, tone)
	return yue_reflex(initial, final, tone, false)
end

-- Expected colloquial (白讀) Cantonese reflex, or nil where the colloquial
-- layer is not expected to differ from predict_yue.
function export.predict_yue_coll(initial, final, tone)
	local colloquial = yue_reflex(initial, final, tone, true)
	if colloquial ~= yue_reflex(initial, final, tone, false) then
		return colloquial
	end
end

The diff against the live module is 56 lines added and 5 removed. Over all 25,609 Middle Chinese readings on Wiktionary, predict_yue and predict_cmn produce exactly what the live module produces. 2,821 readings get a distinct colloquial form.

How many more readings match

The test set is every character that has both Middle Chinese data and Cantonese readings (c= in {{zh-pron}}) on its own page: 12,681 characters with 17,595 distinct attested readings. A reading counts as matched if it equals the prediction for any of the character's Middle Chinese readings.

The colloquial layer adds 392 matched readings, raising coverage from 62.7% to 64.9%, and 259 more characters have all their readings explained. The gain is modest because colloquial forms are lexical: most characters in a 梗攝 rhyme never developed a colloquial reading, so a low attestation rate is expected, not a sign the rule is wrong. The rate still separates the rules. Division II -aang/-aak is attested for 38% of eligible characters, open III–IV -eng/-ek for only 10%.

What Wiktionary's own labels say

A c_note on {{zh-pron}} sometimes labels Cantonese readings, as on 生: saang1 - colloquial; sang1 - literary. Only 136 characters carry such labels, too few to learn from but enough to check against.

The 7 "colloquial" readings that the literary layer predicts mostly come from a different Middle Chinese reading of the same character. 車 ce1 (colloquial) and geoi1 (literary) descend from two separate 廣韻 readings, and the module already predicts both. Wiktionary's label there marks a reading split, not a stratum. The 2 literary-labelled readings caught by the colloquial layer are 舵 to5 and 喫 hek3: forms of our "colloquial" type that serve as the ordinary reading.

The 14 colloquial-labelled readings matched by neither layer point to the next rules to try: 效攝 -iu → -eu (調 deu6, 噍 zeu6), 果攝 -o → -oe (多 doe1, 朵 doe2), plus single-word items such as 婦 pou5 in 新抱.

Open questions

  1. Should the two kinds of rule stack?

    A 梗攝 syllable with a fully voiced initial in the rising tone triggers both rules, and the combined form is almost never attested (1 of 40). The attested forms show one rule or the other:

    CharMCLiteraryCombinedAttested
    挺定青四開 上ding6teng5ting5 ting2 ting4
    梃 娗 誔 涏 鋌定青四開 上ding6teng5ting5
    艇定青四開 上ding6teng5teng5 ting5
    阱從清三開 上zing6ceng5zing6 zeng6 zeng2
    靜 靖 婧從清三開 上zing6ceng5zing6

    The tone-5 aspirate and the low 梗攝 vowel behave as independent layers. Options: return one form per layer (a list instead of one string), or split 全濁上 into its own function.

  2. Is 全濁上 tone 5 colloquial at all?

    Among single-reading characters, 63 attest only the tone-5 aspirate, 168 only the literary tone 6, and 21 both. For words like 舅 kau5, 婢 pei5 and 似 ci5 the "colloquial" form is the only reading, and Wiktionary labels 舵 to5 literary. It may fit better as a vernacular tone layer, separate from the 白讀 rhymes.

  3. Should -aang/-aak be the default in 梗攝 II?

    In voiceless entering syllables of 梗攝 II, the literary prediction -ak is attested 9 times (7 in tone 1); -aak is attested 59 times (40 in tone 3, 10 in tone 1, 9 in tone 6). Across all tones, 86 single-reading characters attest only -aang/-aak and 68 only -ang/-ak. Here the "colloquial" form is the commoner one.

  4. What is in the unmatched 35%?

    Not yet broken down. The samples so far show readings taken from the phonetic component (吸 kap1), 又讀 that 廣韻 does not record, and readings of rare characters. Separating those from true misses in the rules is the next step for both layers.

How this was measured

The Lua modules run unchanged under Lua 5.1 (the version Scribunto uses) through lupa, with a small stand-in for mw.ustring and mw.text written in Python. Lua's own patterns match bytes, so the stand-in translates patterns to Python regular expressions and matches whole characters. For 生 重 白 石 四 坐 話 吃 the local output was compared with what Wiktionary renders and agrees on every reading.

Data comes from the enwiktionary dump of 1 November 2026: 19,843 characters with Middle Chinese modules, 183,342 pages with {{zh-pron}}, and 1,027 dial-pron modules. Module data is evaluated in an empty Lua environment, so it can only build tables.
