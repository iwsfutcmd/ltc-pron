The 溪 initial in Cantonese

Middle Chinese 溪 was an aspirated velar stop, kʰ-. Cantonese usually turned it into h-, or f- before rounded vowels, but about a third of attested readings keep k- or kw-. The working copy of predict_yue keeps k- before 14 finals chosen from the data. This page sets out the evidence and the choice to make before proposing the module upstream. What happened to 溪

溪 is the Middle Chinese initial of 溪 開 口 苦: an aspirated velar stop, kh- in Baxter's transcription, still a stop in Mandarin (kāi, kǒu, kǔ). In Cantonese the stop mostly weakened to h-, and before rounded vowels that h- became f-, so 苦 is fu2 and 科 fo1. The change did not reach every word. h- and f- are one outcome: the module produces f- from h- wherever the rhyme is rounded. Unaspirated g-/gw- (a tenth of readings) and the other outliers are not predicted by any policy below.

What the module does

Upstream, the table debuccalisation_inducing_yue lists all 160 final types, so every 溪 syllable gets h-, and the rounding rule later turns that h- into f- before rounded rhymes other than i. The prediction is h-/f- everywhere.

The working copy takes 14 finals off the list: those where attested k-/kw- outnumber h-/f- at least two to one, with at least three attestations. They are 侯一開、唐一合、唐一合入、唐一開入、山二開入、支三合、江二開入、清三合、脂三合、虞三合、魂一合、麻二合、齊四合、齊四開. (庚三開入 also meets the criterion, but its readings have kw- or gw-, which an open final cannot produce, so it stayed on the list.) Before them 溪 keeps k-, and the same rounding rule gives kw- (誇 kwaa1, 困 kwan3).

The module also contains a rule that turns 溪 into j- before 尤 and 侵/緝, matching 丘 jau1, 欽 jam1 and 泣 jap1. It never runs: it sits in the same if/elseif chain after the h- test, and every final is on the h- list.

	if initial == 29 and debuccalisation_inducing_yue[final] then
		initial_result = "h"
	elseif initial == 29 and (final == 136 or final >= 139 and final <= 142)   -- 尤, 侵, 緝: never reached
		or initial == 32 and (final == 136 or final == 57)
		or initial == 33 and (labialisation_inducing_yue[final] or glide_inducing_yue[final])
		or initial_result == "" and palatalisation_inducing_yue[final] then
		initial_result = "j"
	end

The top of the table is clean (麻二合, 支三合, 清三合, 虞三合 keep the stop every time), and so is the bottom (添, 耕, 戈, 登入 always lose it). The middle is mixed within single finals: 唐一開 has 慷 with both, and 陽三合 splits 框 筐 眶 between kwaang1 and hong1. Many of the clean finals rest on three or four rare characters.

Is there a rule?

Rounding matters most: rounded finals keep the stop in 59% of readings, open ones in 30%. It still does not decide. Rounded 寬 fun1, 科 fo1, 快 faai3 and 恢 fui1 lost it, and open 溪 kai1, 曲 kuk1 and 確 kok3 kept it. Division and tone shift the share less. Rare characters keep k- somewhat more often than common ones, as reading pronunciations of bookish characters would.

Register points the same way. Words that use a 溪 character's k- form are less often vernacular than words that use its h- form: 3.4% of 1,182 tokens against 6.8% of 5,271. There are vernacular k- words, 屋企 and 傾偈 among them, but overall h-/f- is the native outcome and the k- forms lean learned. So the stop is not a colloquial layer either, and does not belong in predict_yue_coll.

A rounding rule recovers only 18 to 32 readings, because rounded finals split. The 14-final list recovers 85, and no list of finals can recover more than 134, since the rest of the k- readings sit in finals where h- is commoner. Making the j- rule reachable adds 11 more, on top of either upstream or the current list.

Recommendation: return to upstream's h-/f- everywhere, and make the j- rule reachable.

This applies the principle already used for 梗攝 II: the literary prediction gives the regular sound change, not the most frequent form. For 溪 the regular change is h-/f-. It is the majority outcome, the native one, and needs no list to justify it. The 14-final list buys 85 readings by recording which words kept the stop; that is lexical information, and a list of finals is a poor place to keep it. The j- rule is different in kind: it is conditioned (尤, 侵 and 緝, before a high front vowel), the module's author meant it to run, and it gains 11 readings without a list.

In code, that is upstream's full debuccalisation_inducing_yue minus finals 136 and 139–142. Literary coverage would go from 63.61% to 63.19%, giving back 74 of the readings the working copy gained.
